# The LinkedIn Headshot Prompt

Turn a normal photo of yourself into a usable LinkedIn headshot. No photographer, no paid headshot app. Took me about 2 and a half minutes.

## How to use it
1. Open **ChatGPT**. Attach **two** photos of yourself — one straight-on, one at a slight angle. Two references hold your likeness far better than one; ChatGPT uses the front-facing one for facial geometry and the angled one for detail.
2. Use clear, well-lit photos. No sunglasses, no heavy filter. **Your source photo is the ceiling** — a blurry input gives you a face that isn't yours.
3. Paste the prompt below, unchanged.

---

## The prompt

> Transform this photo into a professional corporate LinkedIn headshot.
>
> THIS IS A RETOUCH OF AN EXISTING PHOTO, NOT A NEW PERSON. Preserve the subject's exact facial features, bone structure, skin tone, hairline and likeness. Do NOT beautify, idealise or symmetrise the face. Do NOT narrow the jaw, nose or cheeks. Do NOT whiten teeth. Do NOT lighten skin. Do NOT remove moles, marks or under-eye shadows. Keep real skin texture, pores and stubble visible. It must be instantly recognisable as the same person to someone who knows them.
>
> Remove any microphone, mic arm and background clutter. Dress the subject in a premium tailored charcoal-grey blazer over a crisp white shirt, no tie. Clean, softly blurred neutral light-grey studio backdrop. Even softbox lighting from the front-left with a subtle rim light, no harsh shadows.
>
> Expression: confident and approachable, slight natural closed-mouth smile, looking directly into the lens. Head-and-shoulders framing at eye level, vertical portrait crop. Sharp focus on the eyes. Photorealistic, shot on an 85mm lens at f/2.8.

---

## Why the middle paragraph matters

Most AI headshot prompts only say what they *want*. This one spends a whole paragraph on what the model must **not** do — and that's the part doing the work.

Left alone, image models optimise for *attractive*, not *accurate*: they slim the jaw, whiten the teeth, lighten the skin and smooth away every pore. You get a beautiful photo of someone who isn't you. That's a real problem for a picture whose only job is to look like you before a meeting.

Those `Do NOT` lines are the difference between a headshot and a photo of a stranger.

## The honest limits

- **It ignored two of my instructions.** I asked for a slight natural smile and got a stern face. I asked for even softbox lighting and got something moodier and more dramatic. If either matters to you, run it again and repeat *just* those two.
- **It still flatters you slightly** — skin comes out a little cleaner than reality even with the locks in place. Compare against your source before you post it.
- **Glasses, unusual hair and hands** are still where these models break. Re-run rather than accept a bad one.
- **Don't post a version you wouldn't be recognised from.** A headshot that oversells you fails at the first meeting.
